# OssusArchives — Design Context

## Users

Star Wars book readers and collectors. They care about completionism (tracking what they own and have read), discovery (finding books they didn't know existed), and fandom identity (this app should feel like it was made by someone who gets it). Context is casual — browsing on the couch, scanning books at a store or on a shelf at home.

## Brand Personality

**Bold, Playful, Galactic** — but grounded. The interface evokes a Star Wars datapad: functional, slightly futuristic, with personality. No starfield backgrounds or gimmicky space effects — the Star Wars identity comes through color, typography weight, and subtle UI cues rather than literal theming.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Dark mode cinematic with skeuomorphic book covers.**

- Dark surfaces as the default backdrop — books and covers are the visual heroes
- 3D book covers with perspective, spine indent, gradient lighting, and shadow
- The rest of the UI is clean dark surfaces with subtle depth (not flat, not heavily textured)
- Inspiration: Apple Books shelf UI (cover presentation, library feel) + the official Star Wars app (datapad/hologram UI aesthetic)
- Skeuomorphism is reserved for the books themselves and key tactile moments (press states, toggles). Not embossed buttons everywhere.

### Anti-references
- Generic flat Material Design
- Starfield/space backgrounds
- Overly literal Star Wars chrome (Aurebesh everywhere, metallic textures)
- Bright white UI

## Color System

**Primary accent:** Lightsaber blue (`#4A90D9` range — warm Jedi blue, not cold corporate blue). Used for interactive elements: buttons, active tab, toggles, links.

**Secondary accent:** Star Wars yellow (`#FFE81F`). Used sparingly for emphasis: scanner viewfinder, special badges, achievements.

**Canon/Legends badges:** Navy `#1a3a5c` (Canon), warm brown `#5c3a1a` (Legends) — already established, keep these.

**Cover fallback palette:** Deep, muted earth tones — already established in Book3D.tsx.

**Surfaces (dark mode):**
- Background: near-black (`#0A0A0C`)
- Card/elevated: dark gray (`#1A1A1E`)
- Subtle border/divider: `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`

**Text:**
- Primary: `#ECEDEE`
- Secondary: `rgba(255,255,255,0.6)`
- Tertiary/hint: `rgba(255,255,255,0.3)`

**Future:** User-selectable themes (e.g., Sith red, Mandalorian green, Rebel orange). Design the color system as tokens so themes can swap the primary accent.

## Typography

- System fonts (San Francisco on iOS, Roboto on Android) — no custom fonts needed
- Bold weight for titles and emphasis — gives the "bold" part of the personality
- Slightly larger-than-default title sizes for that cinematic feel
- Monospace for ISBNs and technical metadata

## Design Principles

1. **Books are the hero.** Every design decision should make the book covers more prominent. The UI recedes; the collection shines. Dark backgrounds exist to make covers pop.

2. **Tactile, not theatrical.** Skeuomorphism serves function — 3D covers feel like real objects, press states feel like real buttons. But no space sounds, no crawling text, no chrome panels. Restraint is the point.

3. **Collector's pride.** The app should make users feel good about their collection. Counts, progress, visual density of covers — all reinforce "look what I have." The bookshelf is a trophy case.

4. **Galactic identity without cosplay.** The app feels Star Wars through color (lightsaber blue, SW yellow), through confidence (bold typography, dark surfaces), and through subject matter. It doesn't need to dress up as a datapad — it IS one.

5. **Design for theming from day one.** Colors are tokens, not hardcoded hex values. The default Jedi blue theme ships first, but Sith red, Mandalorian green, and others are planned. Every color choice should reference a token, not a literal value.
